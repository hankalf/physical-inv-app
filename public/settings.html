      Supervisor logins everyone who changes something is recorded by name in the log

      Give each supervisor their own login. Admins can manage accounts and everything else; supervisors can do everything except manage accounts. Deactivating a login keeps its history in the log but stops it being used.
      Leave the password blank and one is generated for you — read it out once, and they choose their own the first time they sign in. Type one instead and they still have to replace it, so nobody but its owner ever knows a working password.
      Only an admin can add or remove logins. You can still change your own password below.

      Your password

      You are signed in with the shared password, which has no account to change. Ask an admin for a login of your own.

      What the scanners offer the one-tap reasons a counter can pick, without typing

      The reasons a site needs are the site's own — "Blocked by a trailer" means something here and nothing anywhere else. A scanner picks up a change within about half a minute, without anybody signing out of it.

      Override reasons

      Offered when a pallet ID is not on the list and the counter is allowed to accept it anyway.
      How long the comments step waits before moving on by itself is set on the Scanner screen tab, with the rest of what the counting screen does.

      Adjustment reasons why a count differs from the system — picked when an adjustment is approved

      Used on the dashboard's Adjustments tab, on counts where Adjustments need approval is turned on. They end up in the ERP file beside the number, and in the log — which is what an auditor asking "who wrote off that pallet, and why" is actually asking for. "Other" is always offered with a free-text note.

      Accuracy targets

      What each class has to hit on the Count accuracy report, on counts with ABC classes & accuracy turned on. A items are the handful of SKUs that are most of the throughput, so they are held to the highest number; C items are the long tail.

      Scanner setup each scanner gets its own link — open it once on the device and add it to the home screen

      On the device: open the link in Chrome (or scan the QR into the address bar), then menu → Add to Home screen. Opening the link signs that scanner in — the server accepts counts from signed-in scanners only. Treat a link like a key: Reset link issues a new one and kills the old, and removing a scanner stops it entirely. A link used more than once is flagged, which is normal after a scanner is wiped and worth a look otherwise.

      Barcode test book real Code 128 labels to practise on

      A page of bin and pallet barcodes to print, cut up and scan — for training a crew, or dry-running a count before the real one. The same symbology the racking uses, so a gun that reads the book reads the rack. Take the codes from this count, or fill in the spreadsheet with whatever you want to practise on.

      Scanner

      Bin list every location in the warehouse

      What a scanned bin is checked against, and what defines the aisles teams get assigned to. Upload this one first.
      The ERP bin export that ships with the app: racks F01–F24 and A01–A04 plus WIP, the NIL bin and other areas. Staging lanes and dock doors are counted manually and are left out.

      Inventory report what the system thinks is on hand

      What the count is compared against — it is where variances, second counts and the ERP adjustments come from. Re-upload it whenever the ERP moves on.

      Counting plan which team takes which aisle

      One row per aisle, in the order each team should count it, with the levels their equipment reaches. Optional — aisles can be queued by hand on the Dashboard.

      Aisles & racking blocks

      Aisles in the same block share racking, so only one team can be active in a block at a time. Pair them automatically, or type a block name on any row. Teams are queued onto these aisles on the Dashboard.
